# Setting SOP: Running a Natural Setting Call for Any Offer

A reusable standard operating procedure for qualifying and booking prospects, whatever you're setting for: trading, ecom, Airbnb, credit repair, agencies, coaching, or anything else. Part 1 is the offer-agnostic core. Part 2 shows how to adapt it to a new offer in about an hour. Part 3 has worked offer modules. The 50 example calls in `roleplays/` are trading-specific illustrations of the same method.

---

# PART 1: THE CORE (works for every offer)

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

The framework is a **checklist of buckets to fill, not a list of questions to read.** The prospect's first answer tells you which kind of person you're talking to. You follow what they say, go deep on whatever is loaded, and use short bridge sentences to land in the bucket you still need. By the end you need every bucket filled, a clear gap, an honest read on whether they're a fit, and a locked time. The order changes from call to call. The destination doesn't.

## 2. Core principles

1. **Follow, don't read.** If they answer three buckets at once, skip them and confirm back. Never ask what they've already told you.
2. **Go in when it's loaded.** If someone drops something heavy (divorce, debt, a death, "my relationship is falling apart"), stop the framework and go into it right then. Coming back three questions later feels fake. Loaded answers are usually the real reason.
3. **Classify every answer in your head.**
   - **Vague** ("more money", "freedom", "I want to grow") → dig for the real reason.
   - **Loaded** (60-hour weeks, a failed attempt, a relationship on the edge) → stay on it.
   - **Specific** ("I make $500, I want $7k") → confirm, quantify, move on.
4. **Dig twice, differently.** If the first "why?" gets nothing, try a new angle (what would change, what happens if nothing changes, tell me about the moment). If two tries fail, move to Current Situation and let their life answer it.
5. **Bridge, don't jump.** Every transition reacts to what they just said, then lands in the next bucket.
6. **Confirm back out loud.** Before the gap and after any long answer, say it back and get a yes.
7. **Let them say the problem.** The prospect names the obstacle, not you.
8. **Honest beats booked.** A no-fit lead sent to the closer wastes everyone's time. A kind, honest no keeps the door open.
9. **Never promise results.** No guarantees, no income or outcome claims, no "it only takes minutes a day".
10. **Never help anyone borrow or risk money they can't spare.** Credit cards, loans, retirement funds and emergency funds are hard stops.
11. **Know your role.** You're the first conversation, not the expert. Say so plainly when challenged.
12. **Silence is a tool.** After a loaded answer, wait. Count to four.
13. **Curiosity over persuasion.** Your job is to understand and decide, not to convince.

## 3. The spine

**INTENT → [middle buckets, order depends on the prospect] → SHOW THE GAP → WANT TO CHANGE / URGENCY → QUALIFICATION → PITCH → SCHEDULE & LOCK → COMMITMENT TO SHOW UP**

Intent is always first. Show the Gap through Commitment is always last and always in that order.

### The middle buckets

| Code | Bucket | What you're after | Generic questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| PE | Past Experience | What they've actually tried, with numbers | "Have you done this before? How long? How did it go?" |
| G | Goal | The real goal as a number, and why | "What would it need to look like for this to be worth your time?" |
| EI | Early Impact | The emotional cost of staying where they are | "Why is that important now? What would it let you do that you can't now?" |
| CS | Current Situation | Job, hours, income, resources, how they feel about it | "What do you do? How do you feel about it? How many hours? How long?" |
| TL | Timeline | How long they've been thinking about it, and by when they want it | "How long have you been looking into this? When do you want to be there?" |
| WGW | What Got in the Way | The obstacle, in their words | "What actually stopped you from taking the real step?" |

**EI is a jump bucket, not a fixed slot.** The moment pain surfaces, take it, wherever it appears.

**You can't show the gap until you have G, CS, TL and WGW.** If one is missing, ask for it next.

### The fixed tail

| Step | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Show the gap** | Reflect where they are, where they want to be, and the problem, back to them | "You're in X, you want Y, and what's in the way is Z. Correct?" Use *their* words. |
| **Want to change / urgency** | Test that they want it now | "You told me you want X. Are you actually committed to making that happen now?" |
| **Qualification** | Confirm they're able and willing to invest, and a fit | Money first, then any offer-specific requirements (see Part 2) |
| **Pitch** | Connect them with the closer | Short: what the closer will do, not a sales pitch |
| **Schedule & lock** | Two time options, then email and phone, then accept the invite while you wait | "Tomorrow or [day after]?" "Morning or evening?" "[Time 1] or [Time 2]?" |
| **Commitment to show up** | Prevent the no-show | "If something comes up, message me and we'll reschedule." Pre-call material, a quiet space, the right device |

## 4. The router: reading the first answer

What they say to the intent question tells you which bucket to go into first.

| They say | Go to |
|---|---|
| "I'm new / never done it" | G → CS (beginner path) |
| "I've been researching / watching content" | PE (has anything been live?) |
| "I started but I'm stuck" / "I can't do it myself" | PE right away |
| "It works sometimes, I want it to be consistent" | PE → EI |
| "I want to make more money" | Vague: "Everyone does, why right now?" → G |
| "I want freedom" | Vague: "What would that look like day to day?" → G |
| "I want out of my job" | CS right away |
| "I'm already doing well and want to scale" | PE with numbers, short call |
| "I got burned before" | PE → EI |
| "Just looking / curious" | Two digs, then decide |
| "How much is it?" | Park it, keep going |

**Rule:** go to the bucket their answer points at, then fill the rest.

## 5. How to pick the middle order for any prospect

You don't need to memorise tables. Use these rules in order:

1. **Start with the bucket their first answer points at.** (See the router.)
2. **Experience-heavy prospects** (they've tried before) → PE first, then WGW. The story of what went wrong is the call.
3. **Pain-driven prospects** (stress, pressure, relationship strain) → EI or CS first. The pain is the topic. Don't rush past it.
4. **Beginners** → G and CS first. They have nothing to say about PE, so don't make them.
5. **Scarred or sceptical prospects** (burned before) → PE → EI → WGW. Trust before goals.
6. **High achievers** → PE with numbers, then G, then WGW. Keep it short.
7. **Always** fill G, CS, TL and WGW before showing the gap. EI is optional but take it when it appears.
8. **If they hand you a later bucket unprompted, take it** and skip ahead.

### Default orders by stage (offer-neutral)

| Stage | What they look like | Middle order |
|---|---|---|
| **S0 · Never started** | New, watching from the sidelines | G → CS → EI → TL → WGW |
| **S1 · Researcher** | Consuming content, never acted | PE → G → CS → TL → WGW |
| **S2 · Just started** | Early attempts, little to show | PE → WGW → G → TL → CS |
| **S3 · Stuck** | Tried for a while, can't get traction | PE → G → TL → WGW → CS |
| **S4 · Partial results** | Some results, inconsistent or too small | PE → EI → G → CS → TL → WGW |
| **S5 · Advanced** | Doing well, wants to scale or optimise | PE → G → WGW → TL → CS |
| **S6 · Burned** | Lost money or time to a bad program, advisor or attempt | PE → EI → WGW → G → TL → CS |
| **S7 · Comparing / has a provider** | Already working with someone else | PE → WGW → G → EI → TL → CS |

### Drivers (offer-neutral overlay)

The driver is *why* they want it. It changes which bucket you lead with and what you dig into. When a stage and a driver combine, use the stage's order and put the driver's lead bucket first after PE.

| Driver | Lead bucket | Dig into |
|---|---|---|
| **Escape** (get out of the job or situation) | CS → EI | Hours, relationships, quit vs supplement |
| **Supplement** (extra income, not a replacement) | G → CS | How much extra, for what |
| **Pressure** (debt, bills, urgency) | EI → CS | How heavy it is. **Check qualification early.** |
| **Family** (providing, being present) | G → EI | What they're missing, who depends on them |
| **Lifestyle / status** | G → CS | The real why under the flex |
| **Location / time freedom** | G → CS | Cost of the current setup |
| **Business owner** (diversifying) | CS → G | Hours available, why this and not their business |
| **High earner, time-poor** | CS → G | Realistic hours, reschedule risk |
| **Capital holder** | PE → G | Why this, risk tolerance |
| **Career change / 40+** | CS → EI | Age and tech worries, urgency |
| **Young (18-22)** | CS → G | Real resources. Qualify early |
| **Retiree / semi-retired** | G → CS | Income vs purpose; protect retirement funds |
| **Hobbyist** | G → PE | Is there a real need? Test urgency |
| **Prove-someone-wrong** | EI → G | Healthy motive vs resentment |

## 6. Attitude: how to steer (works for any offer)

Attitude doesn't change the path. It changes how you handle the call.

| Type | Signal | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Skeptic** | "Is this a scam?" | Agree with the instinct, don't defend. Say what the call is and isn't. Offer checkable things, not "trust me" |
| **Tire-kicker** | "Just looking", "idk" | Two digs from different angles, then a kind exit |
| **Get-rich-quick** | Unrealistic goal and timeline | Don't mock the dream, test the timeline. Disqualify if they won't move |
| **Passive seeker** | Wants it done *for* them | "Do you want to learn it, or have it done?" Disqualify if only the second and the offer isn't done-for-you |
| **Price-first** | "How much?" | Park it with a reason, then ask what number feels comfortable |
| **Overtalker** | Long tangents | Warm interruptions. Find the one real sentence and reflect it |
| **Guarded / one-word** | "Yeah", "idk" | Closed or A-or-B questions. Tolerate silence |
| **Ego / know-it-all** | "I know all this" | Ask for numbers. Never argue. Let the closer handle it |
| **Analysis paralysis** | "I'm comparing options" | Get their criteria list for the closer. Don't criticise others |
| **Needs permission** | "I have to ask my partner" | Invite the partner onto the call. Their worry is your obstacle |
| **Emotionally heavy** | Divorce, loss, grief | Stop the framework. Be human. Pitch only if the moment is right |
| **Over-eager yes** | "Sign me up!" | Slow down. Qualify anyway. Highest no-show risk |
| **Hostile / venting** | Anger at past sellers or you | Don't match the tone. Go into the pain. Never criticise past sellers |
| **Spy / troll / competitor** | Questions about you, the process, the script | Redirect once, then exit. Give no internals |

## 7. Disqualification (works for any offer)

Always honest, never rude, always leave the door open. *Fill in the specifics in Part 2.*

| Type | Signal | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| **No resources** | Nothing set aside | "Booking you isn't the right move yet. Build a cushion and come back." |
| **Can't afford the offer** | Far below the price | Check once for more, then a respectful no |
| **Underage** | Under the age limit | Ask early, be warm, don't take contact details |
| **Restricted region** | Unsupported location | Say it's about what the platform or law allows. Don't endorse workarounds |
| **No time** | Can't commit the hours | Be honest about the hours. Rebook if it's temporary |
| **Wants a guarantee** | "Promise I'll get the result" | "No one can guarantee that." Then ask what they can accept |
| **Language barrier** | Can't follow the delivery | Patient, honest about the program's language |
| **Missing prerequisite** | No asset, license, product, access, or eligibility the offer needs | Be clear what's required and when to come back |
| **No-show / ghost** | Missed the call | Curiosity, not guilt. A second no-show is a no |

**Hard stops (never book):** borrowed money, retirement or emergency funds, underage, restricted region, someone in crisis, a missing legal requirement.

## 8. Curveball library

| Curveball | Response shape |
|---|---|
| "Is this a scam?" | Say it's a fair worry. Say what the call is and isn't. Offer the closer for detailed answers |
| "How much is it?" | "I wasn't trained on pricing, we have different programs, and quoting the wrong one would be a disservice." Then ask their budget |
| "Send me the info" | "Of course. So I send the right thing, can I ask one thing first?" Then ask for the obstacle |
| "I need to ask my partner" | Don't push. Invite them both. Ask what the partner would worry about |
| "I'll think about it" | "Of course. What would you be thinking about?" It's usually money, a person, or doubt |
| "I'm busy right now" | Offer a shorter version, or lock a specific time. Never leave it open |
| "What are your results?" | "I'm not the [expert], I'm the first conversation. You'd judge [CLOSER], not me." |
| "I'm already with another provider" | Don't criticise them. Ask what's missing |
| "I already know this" | Ask for numbers. Don't argue |
| "Can I use my retirement money?" | No. Clear, kind, final |
| Emotional break | Don't fill the silence. One human sentence, then wait |
| They go silent | Count to four, then ask an easier, more specific question |
| Technical question | "That's [CLOSER]'s area." Park it in one line |
| Time-zone confusion | Confirm out loud and check the invite |

## 9. Bridge bank

| From → to | Example |
|---|---|
| Vague money → G | "Everyone wants more, I've never met someone who wants less. Why right now?" |
| Freedom → G | "Freedom means different things. What would that look like in your day to day?" |
| G → EI | "What would that let you do that you can't do right now?" |
| CS → EI | "What does that schedule do to your week, your relationships?" |
| CS → TL | "How long have you been looking into [topic]?" |
| TL (long) → WGW | "That's [X months]. How come you haven't started?" |
| TL (short) → WGW | "Props for acting fast. What do you think might stop you?" |
| PE → WGW | "Looking back, what do you think went wrong?" |
| WGW → gap | "You're in X, you want Y, and what's in the way is Z. Correct?" |
| Gap → urgency | "You told me you want X. Are you actually committed to making that happen now?" |
| Urgency → qualification | "If there was a way to get you there, would you be ready to invest in yourself?" |
| Qualification → pitch | "Here's what I can do. I'll connect you with [CLOSER]..." |

## 10. Gap statement and handoff

> "So from what I understand, you're [current situation] and want to get to [desired situation], and the thing stopping you is [problem], correct?"

**Handoff notes for the closer (log after every booking):**
- name, stage, driver, attitude, and the path used
- the real goal in their words, with the number
- the emotional why
- the obstacle in their words
- budget stated, plus flags (tight, borrowed, spouse)
- offer-specific qualifiers (see Part 2)
- urgency (high, medium or low) and why
- objections raised
- time zone and booked slot
- anything personal to reference

## 11. Safety and ethics (all offers)

- **No outcome or income claims, no guarantees.** Say what's true.
- **No borrowed money, retirement or emergency funds.** Ever.
- **Be honest about risk and effort.**
- **Someone in crisis comes before the call.** If they mention hurting themselves, stop, be human, and point them to appropriate crisis resources for their region.
- **Respect "no".** One reframe, then let it go.
- **Don't criticise competitors or past sellers.**
- **Know the claims and fee rules for your industry and region** and stay inside them. This matters most for credit repair, finance, real estate and health.

## 12. Checklists

- **Before:** read the form or CRM notes, check the time zone, pick your opener.
- **During:** keep the buckets visible, tick them off, note their exact words for the gap.
- **After:** log the stage, gap, budget, qualifiers, flags and next step.

# PART 3: WORKED OFFER MODULES

Each module is Part 2 filled in. Use them as a starting point and edit against your real offer.

## Module A · Trading / futures (build-income)

**Stages:**
- S0 never traded, S1 learning from videos, S2 small live account, S3 can't get consistent, S4 profitable but small or inconsistent, S5 full-time and scaling, S6 burned by a course or blown account, S7 already with a mentor.

**Typical obstacles:** no system, emotional trading, no feedback on trades, information overload, fear of losing money, not knowing whom to trust.

**Qualification:** capital that can be risked, hours available, region the broker and platform support, age 18+.

**Hard stops:** borrowed money, retirement or emergency funds, underage, unsupported region.

**Offer-specific disqualifiers:** signal seekers (want trades done for them), get-rich-quick expectations, people who want guarantees.

**Handoff fields:** account size, markets traded, results with numbers, drawdown history, prior courses bought.

**Listening for:** the difference between "I know the concepts" and "I can execute", and how losses have affected them emotionally.

## Module B · Ecommerce / dropshipping / private label (build-income)

**Stages:**
- S0 never launched a store, S1 researching products, S2 store built but no sales, S3 some sales but unprofitable, S4 profitable but small or plateaued, S5 scaling a winning product, S6 burned by a course or an agency, S7 working with a mentor or agency.

**Typical obstacles:** no winning product, bad ad spend, no traffic, low conversion, supplier problems, cash flow, no system or mentor, overwhelm.

**Qualification:** budget for the course and for starting inventory and ads, hours per week, payment-processor access in their country, age 18+.

**Hard stops:** borrowed money, retirement funds, rent or food money, unsupported region.

**Offer-specific disqualifiers:** wants passive income with no work, wants to copy a guru's store, no budget to start.

**Handoff fields:** store link, monthly revenue and profit, ad spend, platform (Shopify, Amazon), niche, what they've tried.

**Listening for:** the gap between "I have a store" and "I have a product people want", and whether their budget covers both the program and a real test.

## Module C · Airbnb / short-term rentals (build-income)

**Stages:**
- S0 interested, no property, S1 researching markets, S2 has a property or lease, not yet listed, S3 listed with few bookings, S4 one property performing, wants more, S5 multiple properties, wants to scale or systemise, S6 burned by arbitrage or a bad market, S7 working with a mentor or manager.

**Typical obstacles:** capital for deposits and furnishing, landlord permission, local regulations, finding a good market, low occupancy, pricing, cleaning and operations, financing.

**Qualification:** capital for deposit and setup, local regulations allow short-term rentals, credit or financing situation, time available, age 18+.

**Hard stops:** borrowed money for deposit, regulations that forbid it where they live, retirement funds.

**Offer-specific disqualifiers:** wants fast income without capital, no access to a property or market, doesn't want to deal with guests.

**Handoff fields:** location or market, property or lease status, capital available, occupancy and revenue (if listed), regulations checked or not.

**Listening for:** which constraint is the real blocker (capital, a property, regulations, operations), and whether they understand the work.

## Module D · Credit repair, two variants

### D1 · Consumer service (fix-a-problem)

A person wants their own credit fixed.

**Stages:**
- S0 knows credit is bad, hasn't done anything, S1 researching, S2 tried disputing on their own, S3 worked with another company, no results, S4 partial improvement, wants more, S5 near goal, wants the last step, S6 burned by a company, S7 comparing providers.

**Typical drivers:** wants a mortgage, a car loan or a lower rate, denied recently, a life change, embarrassment.

**Typical obstacles:** doesn't know what's on the report, collections or judgments, past mistakes, not enough time, distrust of credit repair companies, upfront fee fears.

**Qualification:** what they want the credit for, a rough score range, whether there are open collections or judgments, ability to pay the service fee, region eligibility, and anything the law requires before work begins.

**Hard stops:** anything illegal (fake identities, "new credit profiles"), promises of specific score increases, upfront fees where they're prohibited, no real credit issues to address.

**Handoff fields:** goal (loan type and timeline), approximate scores, negative items, prior repair attempts, budget.

**Listening for:** the real deadline (a house closing, a lease), and what happened with prior companies.

**Compliance:** many regions strictly regulate fee timing and claims in credit repair. Never promise score increases or deletions. Check your regional rules before taking a call.

### D2 · Business model (build-income)

A person wants to *start a credit repair business or agency*.

Use the Build-income pattern from Module B, swapping "store" for "client base" and "product" for "service". Obstacles are finding clients, compliance, dispute process, pricing and software. Qualification adds licensing or registration requirements and budget for tools and marketing.

# PART 4: PRACTICE

1. Pick one stage and one driver from Part 1, pick an offer from Part 3, and write the prospect's hidden truth in one line.
2. Pair up. One plays the prospect with that hidden truth and one curveball. The other runs the call.
3. After the call, check: did you follow the person or the script? Did you fill G, CS, TL and WGW? Did you qualify honestly?
4. Change one variable (the budget, the attitude, the objection) and run it again.

The `roleplays/` folder has 50 worked trading calls as examples of tone, bridges and handling. The structure carries over to any offer. Only the stage details, obstacles and qualification change.
