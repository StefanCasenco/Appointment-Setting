# Top 5 Most Likely Trading Calls (conversational version)

The five conversations you're most likely to get from people who download a futures-trading guide, written to *sound like a chat*, not a form. They follow `../SETTING-SOP.md` (same buckets, same spine) but with a lighter touch, based on feedback from live role-plays:

- **Be upbeat and warm.** A flat, composed tone reads as a robot. Go about 10% livelier than feels natural.
- **React before you ask.** Every question gets a short bridge first ("Ah nice", "Gotcha", "Damn, okay").
- **Every question comes out of their last answer.** If you can't point to the sentence it came from, don't ask it.
- **Peel the pain 2-3 layers, then stop.** Echo their words, ask "what does that look like?", ask "how so?". You're a setter, not a closer: find the problem, don't hold them accountable for it.
- **Keep it short.** 6-8 minutes. If you've asked more than about 8 questions, you've drifted into a discovery call.
- **Sell the meeting, not the product.** You only need to know: is there a real problem, and can we plausibly help? If yes, book. If not, say so kindly.

Same conventions as the other roleplays: `> [NODE: ...]` marks the framework bucket, `> CURVEBALL: ...` marks a surprise, `> PEEL: ...` marks the digging moves. The call lines are the *shape* of the conversation, not a script to memorise.

## Bridge cheat sheet

| Use | Phrases |
|---|---|
| React | "Ah, nice." · "Gotcha." · "Right, okay." · "Ha, fair enough." · "Damn, yeah." · "That makes sense." |
| Connect to what they said | "You said earlier [X], is that part of it?" · "Funny you say that, because..." · "Going back to [X]..." |
| Dig | "What does that look like day to day?" · "How so?" · "How come?" · "How's that sitting with you?" |
| Move on | "Cool, so..." · "Out of curiosity..." · "And while we're on it..." |
| Soft money question | "Roughly, is there something set aside you'd put towards this if it made sense?" |

Don't open every question with "Let me ask you...". Vary it, or just ask.

## 1 · The beginner who's sick of his job

**Path (S0, Escape):** INTENT → CS → EI → G → TL → WGW → GAP → URGENCY → QUAL → PITCH → LOCK → SHOW UP
**Lead:** Daniel, 27, warehouse operative. Has watched trading videos for a few months. No account.
**Hidden truth:** He's exhausted and feels like he's missing his kid's early years.
**Curveball:** asks "is this like a get-rich thing?" halfway through.

### The call

**Ștefan:** Hey Daniel, it's Ștefan from [COMPANY]. How's it going?

**Daniel:** Yeah, good, you?

**Ștefan:** Good, good. You grabbed our futures guide earlier, right? Did it come through alright?

**Daniel:** Yeah, I got it. Haven't properly read it yet.

**Ștefan:** Ha, no stress. I've got a few minutes, just wanted to ask a couple of quick things, see if what we do makes sense for you. And if it doesn't, no worries, I'll point you somewhere useful. That cool?

**Daniel:** Yeah, go on.

**Ștefan:** What made you grab it? What's going on with trading for you?

**Daniel:** I've been watching a lot of videos. Just thought I'd see where it goes. Want to make a bit more.

> **[NODE: INTENT]** Vague ("a bit more"). Don't chase the money yet. Go to what his life looks like, the router says "make more money" goes to G, but "I'm watching videos at home" points to CS first.

**Ștefan:** Gotcha. What are you doing for work at the minute?

**Daniel:** Warehouse. Nights, mostly.

**Ștefan:** Nights, oh man. How's that going?

**Daniel:** It's alright. It's tiring.

> **PEEL (layer 1):** echo, then "what does that look like".

**Ștefan:** Tiring? What's that look like day to day?

**Daniel:** I finish at six in the morning, sleep till two, and by then my little lad's home from nursery. Then I'm back at work at nine. I barely see him.

> **[NODE: EI, jump bucket]** He just handed you the real reason. Stop the framework. Slow down.

**Ștefan:** *(pause, two seconds)* Damn, yeah. That's rough. How old is he?

**Daniel:** Three.

**Ștefan:** And how's that sitting with you, not seeing him much?

> **PEEL (layer 2):** feeling, not facts.

**Daniel:** Honestly? It's the worst part. I feel like I'm missing it.

**Ștefan:** Yeah, I can imagine. And how long's it been like this?

**Daniel:** Couple of years.

> **[NODE: CS + TL, folded in]** One question covers both: how long he's been in it.

**Ștefan:** So where does trading come in? Is it a way out of nights, or more of an extra on top?

**Daniel:** A way out. If I could make enough to drop the nights, even just do days.

**Ștefan:** What would "enough" look like, roughly?

**Daniel:** Like two and a half, three grand a month.

> **[NODE: G]** Specific number, great. Don't query it.

**Ștefan:** Okay, and how long have you been looking into it?

**Daniel:** Maybe six months, watching videos.

**Ștefan:** Six months. What's been the hold-up from going further than the videos?

> **[NODE: WGW]** Light version. Not "why haven't you acted", just the obstacle in his words.

**Daniel:** Honestly I don't know where to start. There's so much stuff, everyone says something different. And I don't want to lose money I haven't got.

> **CURVEBALL: skeptic note.**

**Daniel:** Is this one of those get-rich things? I'm not after a lambo, I just want to see my son.

**Ștefan:** Ha, fair question, and no. Nobody can promise you a result in trading, and I'd be wary of anyone who does. What we do is give you a proper structure and someone to look at your work, so you're not piecing it together from videos. Whether that fits you is what I'm trying to work out. Does that make sense?

**Daniel:** Yeah, that's fair.

> **[NODE: GAP, in his words]**

**Ștefan:** Right, so from what I'm hearing: you're on nights, barely seeing your lad, you want to get to maybe three grand a month so you can move to days and be around, and what's been in the way is not knowing where to start with so much noise out there. Fair?

**Daniel:** Yeah. That's it.

**Ștefan:** And is this something you want to sort now, or more of a someday thing?

**Daniel:** Now. I don't want to look back in two years and still be doing nights.

> **[NODE: QUAL, soft]** No borrowing. Money first, then time, then region.

**Ștefan:** Makes sense. Roughly, have you got something set aside you'd put towards this if it made sense? Not borrowed money, I mean savings.

**Daniel:** A bit. Couple of grand, maybe.

**Ștefan:** Okay, good to know. And what kind of time could you give it in a week, around the shifts?

**Daniel:** I could do an hour or two most days before work.

**Ștefan:** Perfect. Honestly, that sounds like exactly who we work with. Here's what I'd suggest: a call with [CLOSER] on our team. He'll look at where you're at, map out what a sensible plan looks like, and you'll leave with something useful either way. Sound fair?

**Daniel:** Yeah, go on then.

**Ștefan:** Cool. Are you better tomorrow or the day after?

**Daniel:** Day after.

**Ștefan:** Morning or evening?

**Daniel:** Afternoon, before I go in. Say 3?

**Ștefan:** Locked in, 3pm. Best email for the invite? ... And a number for a reminder text? ... Can you accept it while I'm on?

**Daniel:** Done.

**Ștefan:** If anything comes up, just message me and we'll shift it. Find somewhere quiet, ideally a computer, and he'll walk you through it.

### Why this works

- He answered in his own words three times ("tiring", "missing it", "so much noise"), and each answer became the next question.
- The money number was one question, asked flat.
- The "is this a get-rich thing" curveball was answered honestly, with no promise.
- Total: about 11 questions, but several were one-liners ("How old is he?").

### Handoff note

Stage S0, driver Escape. Goal: ~£3k/month so he can leave nights. Why: seeing his 3-year-old son. Obstacle: no clear starting point, fear of losing money. Budget: a couple of grand savings (not borrowed). Time: 1-2 hours before shifts. Skeptic about "get-rich" claims. Booked day-after-tomorrow at 3pm.

## 2 · The paper trader stuck on "my strategy isn't ready"

**Path (S1/S2):** INTENT → PE → CS → G → TL → WGW → GAP → URGENCY → QUAL → PITCH → LOCK → SHOW UP
**Lead:** Jordan, 23, bricklayer. Paper trading for six months, on and off. Did a free bootcamp.
**Hidden truth:** He's not short on knowledge. He's scared to put real money in and uses "my strategy isn't ready" as the reason.
**Curveball:** "I just need to get my strategy down first."

### The call

**Ștefan:** Hey Jordan, it's Ștefan from [COMPANY], how are you doing?

**Jordan:** Good mate, you?

**Ștefan:** Good. You grabbed our futures guide earlier, right?

**Jordan:** Yeah, I signed up like ten minutes ago.

**Ștefan:** Ha, brilliant timing. I've only got a few minutes, but I wanted to ask a couple of quick things and see if there's a fit. If not, no stress. Alright?

**Jordan:** Yeah, go for it.

**Ștefan:** What got you into trading?

**Jordan:** Money, mostly. Want to look after my family, give them more.

> **[NODE: INTENT]** "Money" is vague. Don't say "money, right?" (that leads him). Bridge past it instead.

**Ștefan:** Fair enough. Are you doing it already, or still getting your head round it?

**Jordan:** Paper trading for about six months. Known about it for a year.

> **[NODE: PE]** Router: "already doing it" → PE.

**Ștefan:** Six months. Nice, that's more than most. What changed, that you decided to start?

**Jordan:** I got sick of sitting on the sidelines. You can watch a trader make 100k on YouTube every day, but it doesn't mean anything until you try.

**Ștefan:** Ha, true. And how's the paper trading going?

**Jordan:** Honestly, not great. I'm on and off, so I'm still really a beginner. I don't have a strategy down.

> **PEEL (layer 1):** echo the feeling, ask for the picture.

**Ștefan:** Not having a strategy. What does that look like when you're actually sitting there?

**Jordan:** I'll take trades that feel right, then I'll second guess. Or I'll watch someone's video and change everything.

**Ștefan:** Gotcha, so a bit of everyone's advice, and none of it sticking. How's that feeling?

**Jordan:** Frustrating. I put in the hours and I'm not getting anywhere.

> **[NODE: CS, light]** Slide it in as a normal question, not an interview.

**Ștefan:** Totally. What are you doing for work, alongside it?

**Jordan:** Construction. Bricklaying.

**Ștefan:** Long hours?

**Jordan:** Fifty, sixty, with the overtime. It's hard on my body.

**Ștefan:** Yeah, I bet. Do you enjoy it?

**Jordan:** Not really. It's just trading my time for money.

> **[NODE: EI, EARNED]** Don't ask "why is it important". He already told you.

**Ștefan:** Right. So what would you want trading to do for you?

**Jordan:** Replace it. Eventually. Maybe five grand a month. I make about three now.

**Ștefan:** And why five rather than, say, three or four?

**Jordan:** I'm 23 but I want a partner and kids at some point. I'd like to be set up before then.

**Ștefan:** Makes sense. So you've been at it six months. What do you reckon's been the hold-up, going from paper to real?

> **[NODE: WGW]** One question, then listen.

**CURVEBALL: "I just need my strategy sorted first."**

**Jordan:** I just need to get my strategy down first.

**Ștefan:** Yeah, that's a common one. If you did have a strategy you trusted tomorrow, would you go live straight away?

> **PEEL (layer 2):** tests whether strategy is the real obstacle without calling him out.

**Jordan:** *(pause)* ...Honestly, probably not straight away. I'd still be nervous about losing real money.

**Ștefan:** Ah, okay. That's honest. So it's partly the strategy and partly that first step of real money?

**Jordan:** Yeah. Both.

> **[NODE: GAP, in his words]**

**Ștefan:** So just so I've got it right: you're bricklaying, 50-60 hours, six months into paper trading, want to get to about 5k a month eventually, and what's in the way is no solid strategy plus being nervous about going live. Right?

**Jordan:** Yeah.

**Ștefan:** Is this something you want to get moving on now, or more of a down-the-line thing?

**Jordan:** Now. I'm tired of waiting.

**Ștefan:** Good. Roughly, have you got a bit set aside you'd put towards getting proper support, if it made sense? Not borrowed.

**Jordan:** Couple of hundred, maybe. A bit more if I had to.

> **Flag for the closer:** budget-sensitive. Don't pitch a number. The closer decides what fits.

**Ștefan:** Okay, thanks for being straight. What I'd suggest is a call with [CLOSER]. He'll look at where you're at and what a sensible next step looks like, and if it's a fit he'll talk you through options. Fair?

**Jordan:** Yeah, sounds good.

**Ștefan:** Are you better midweek or the weekend?

**Jordan:** Weekend.

**Ștefan:** Saturday or Sunday? ... Morning or afternoon? ... 11 or 12? ... Locked in. Best email? ... Number for a reminder? ... Accept it while I wait?

**Jordan:** Done.

**Ștefan:** If something comes up, message me. Find somewhere quiet, ideally a computer.

### Why this works

- The "strategy" excuse was tested with one gentle hypothetical, not challenged.
- Bricklaying came up naturally, no "what do you do for a living" jolt.
- No accountability pile-on. We got the obstacle in his words and moved on.

### Handoff note

Stage S1/S2, driver Escape. Goal ~5k/month to replace construction. Obstacle: no confident strategy, nervous about real money. Budget: low, couple of hundred (flag). Hours: 50-60/week at work. Booked Saturday 11.

## 3 · The prop-firm chaser who keeps failing challenges

**Path (S4/S7):** INTENT → PE → EI → WGW → G → TL → CS → GAP → URGENCY → QUAL → PITCH → LOCK → SHOW UP
**Lead:** Marcus, 29, IT support. Has bought and failed three evaluations (about $450 total). Watches a lot of ICT/SMC videos.
**Hidden truth:** He's trading a challenge like a lottery ticket and breaking his own rules when he's down. He's embarrassed about the cost.
**Curveball:** "It's not my fault, the platform glitched."

### The call

**Ștefan:** Hey Marcus, it's Ștefan from [COMPANY]. You've got a couple of minutes?

**Marcus:** Yeah, sure.

**Ștefan:** You grabbed our futures guide. I just wanted to ask a few quick things and see if there's a fit. No pressure either way. Good?

**Marcus:** Yeah.

**Ștefan:** What's your trading story? What brought you to the guide?

**Marcus:** I've been trying to get funded. Tried a few prop firm challenges. Not got through yet.

> **[NODE: INTENT → PE]** "Tried a few" = PE with numbers. Be curious, not judgemental.

**Ștefan:** Ah okay, so you're already in it. How many have you had a go at?

**Marcus:** Three.

**Ștefan:** And how'd they go?

**Marcus:** Passed phase one on one, blew the next one two days later. The other two I hit the daily loss.

> **PEEL (layer 1):** ask for the moment.

**Ștefan:** What happened in those last couple of days, before you hit the limit?

**Marcus:** I was close to the target and started pushing. Taking extra trades to get there quicker.

**Ștefan:** And then?

**Marcus:** Then I broke my rules, revenge traded, and hit the limit.

> **[NODE: EI, EARNED]** Don't rush past this. He just said the real problem.

**Ștefan:** Yeah, that's so common, honestly. How did that feel?

**Marcus:** Annoying. Embarrassing, honestly. That's about $450 gone, and I knew what to do.

**Ștefan:** *(pause)* Yeah. Knowing what to do and doing it under pressure are two different things.

**CURVEBALL: deflection.**

**Marcus:** Though one of them was the platform glitching on my stop.

**Ștefan:** Ha, that's annoying when it happens. Did that one change the result, or would the pattern have shown up anyway?

> Don't argue. Let him answer it himself.

**Marcus:** ...Probably would've happened anyway. I'd already had a bad week.

**Ștefan:** Gotcha. So if you'd fix one thing, what would it be?

> **[NODE: WGW]** His own words on the obstacle: discipline under pressure.

**Marcus:** Sticking to the plan when I'm close. Not getting greedy.

**Ștefan:** Makes sense. And what are you hoping to get out of being funded?

**Marcus:** Trade for a living, eventually. Maybe $4-5k a month to start. I work IT support now, it's fine but it's not going anywhere.

> **[NODE: G + CS, together]** Specific number plus the job in one answer.

**Ștefan:** How long have you been at the trading side of it?

**Marcus:** About a year and a half, off and on.

**Ștefan:** So eighteen months, three challenges, and the thing that keeps tripping you is the rules under pressure. Right?

> **[NODE: GAP, in his words]**

**Marcus:** Yeah, that's it.

**Ștefan:** And do you want to sort this now, or keep running challenges and see?

**Marcus:** Honestly, I don't want to keep burning money. I want to fix it.

**Ștefan:** Makes sense. Roughly, have you got something set aside you'd put toward proper coaching, separate from your challenge budget? Not borrowed.

**Marcus:** Some savings, yeah. Depends what it is.

**Ștefan:** Totally fair, that's what the next call's for. I'd suggest a call with [CLOSER]. He'll look at your last few challenges, where it's going wrong, and what a sensible next step is. If it's a fit he'll talk you through options. Sound alright?

**Marcus:** Yeah, that sounds good.

**Ștefan:** Are you better tomorrow or Thursday?

**Marcus:** Thursday evening.

**Ștefan:** 6 or 7? ... 7 it is. Best email? ... Number for a text reminder? ... Can you accept the invite while I'm here?

**Marcus:** Done.

**Ștefan:** If something comes up, message me. It'd help to have your last few trades or journal to hand if you've got them.

### Why this works

- We never told him he was failing. He said "revenge traded" himself.
- The glitch excuse was answered with a question, not a counter.
- The embarrassment got a human sentence and a pause, not a pitch.

### Handoff note

Stage S4/S7, driver Escape. Three failed evals (~$450). Obstacle: breaks rules near the target and when down (revenge trading). Goal ~$4-5k/month. Embarrassed about wasted money. Has savings, amount unknown. Wants to stop burning challenge fees. Booked Thursday 7pm. Suggest he brings his trade journal.

## 4 · The price-first skeptic

**Path (S0/S1 + Skeptic/Price-first):** INTENT → CS → G → TL → WGW → GAP → URGENCY → QUAL → PITCH → LOCK → SHOW UP
**Lead:** Priya, 34, marketing manager. Burned by an online course years ago in a different niche. Opens with "how much is this?".
**Hidden truth:** She's not against paying. She's afraid of being scammed again and needs to feel the call is honest.
**Curveball:** "Is this a scam?"

### The call

**Ștefan:** Hey Priya, it's Ștefan from [COMPANY]. You grabbed the futures guide earlier, right?

**Priya:** Yeah. Before we start, how much is this going to cost me?

> **CURVEBALL: price up front.** Park it warmly, with a reason. Don't dodge.

**Ștefan:** Ha, straight to it, I like that. Honest answer: I'm the first conversation, I wasn't trained on pricing, and there are different programs depending on where people are at. If I threw a number at you, I'd probably give you the wrong one. Do you mind if I ask a couple of things first so I can point you right?

**Priya:** Hm. Okay. But I don't want to be sold to.

**Ștefan:** Good, because I'm not here to. If it's not a fit, I'll say so. What got you looking at trading?

> **[NODE: INTENT]**

**Priya:** I've been curious for a while. A friend of mine does it and seems to be doing well. But honestly, I'm wary. I did a course years ago, wasted money, and I'm not doing that again.

> **PEEL (layer 1):** she gave you the story. Stay on it.

**Ștefan:** Fair enough. What happened with the course?

**Priya:** It was all hype. Videos, no real help, and when I asked questions I got a sales page.

**Ștefan:** Ugh, that's frustrating. Yeah, I'd be wary too. What would you need to see to feel okay about this one?

> **PEEL (layer 2):** turns her worry into her criteria. Gold for the closer.

**Priya:** Someone actually talking to me. Real people. Not a hype page. And being honest about risk.

**Ștefan:** That's fair, and I can promise you that on this call. Nobody can guarantee results in trading, and anyone who does is lying. What I can say is you'd be talking to a person. Where's trading sitting for you right now? Are you doing anything yet, or still curious?

> **CURVEBALL: "Is this a scam?"**

**Priya:** Is this one of those things where you tell me I'll make loads of money?

**Ștefan:** Ha, no. Trading can lose you money, and that's the risk, full stop. I'd rather you hear that from me. Does that help?

**Priya:** It does, actually. Okay.

> **[NODE: CS + G, folded together]**

**Ștefan:** Cool. What are you doing at the minute, work-wise?

**Priya:** Marketing manager. It's fine. Decent salary. I just want a second income that isn't tied to my job.

**Ștefan:** Right, so more of an extra than a replacement?

**Priya:** Yeah. If I could add a few hundred a week, that'd be great. I'd be happy with that.

**Ștefan:** Few hundred a week, what would that change?

**Priya:** More security. I'm saving for a house and it'd speed things up.

**Ștefan:** Nice, that's a clear goal. How long have you been thinking about trading?

**Priya:** About a year, on and off.

**Ștefan:** A year. What's held you back from starting?

> **[NODE: WGW]**

**Priya:** Honestly, fear of getting it wrong again. And not knowing who to trust.

> **[NODE: GAP, in her words]**

**Ștefan:** So just to check I've got it: you work in marketing, you want a second income to speed up your house savings, a few hundred a week, and what's stopped you is not knowing who to trust after the last course. Is that right?

**Priya:** Yeah. That's it.

**Ștefan:** Do you want to get moving on this now, or are you still exploring?

**Priya:** I'd like to get moving, as long as it's the right thing.

**Ștefan:** Totally fair. Roughly, have you got a bit set aside you'd put toward it if it was the right fit? I'm asking so we don't waste your time, not to price you.

**Priya:** Yeah, I do. I don't want to say a number yet.

> Respect it. She doesn't have to say a number on this call. Note "has budget, guarded about the figure".

**Ștefan:** No problem at all. Here's what I'd suggest. A call with [CLOSER]. He'll answer all of the pricing and "what's the catch" questions properly, with no pressure. And if it's not for you, you've lost nothing. Would that work?

**Priya:** Yes, that'd be good. I'd want to ask him a lot of questions.

**Ștefan:** Perfect. Write them down and bring them, he'll answer everything. Tomorrow or the day after?

**Priya:** Day after, evening.

**Ștefan:** 6 or 7? ... 6. Best email? ... Number for a reminder? ... Can you accept the invite while I'm here?

**Priya:** Done.

### Why this works

- "How much?" was parked with a reason, not dodged.
- We turned her fear into her list of criteria, which is useful for the closer.
- No hype, no promise, and we named the risk ourselves.
- We accepted "I don't want to say a number yet".

### Handoff note

Stage S0/S1, attitude Skeptic/Price-first, driver Supplement. Goal: a few hundred a week to speed up house savings. Burned by a previous course (different niche). Criteria: real person, honest about risk, no hype. Budget exists, number not given (don't push). Bring a list of questions. Booked day-after-tomorrow at 6pm.

## 5 · The one under money pressure

**Path (Pressure driver, ends without booking):** INTENT → EI → CS → G → TL → QUAL → HONEST EXIT
**Lead:** Chloe, 26, retail assistant. Behind on rent and overdraft. Wants trading to fix it fast.
**Hidden truth:** She has nothing to risk and is hoping trading is a quick fix. Booking her would be doing her a disservice.
**Curveball:** "Can I put it on a credit card?"

### The call

**Ștefan:** Hey Chloe, it's Ștefan from [COMPANY]. You grabbed the futures guide earlier, right?

**Chloe:** Yeah. I really need this to work.

**Ștefan:** Ah okay, tell me a bit about that. What's going on?

> **[NODE: INTENT → EI]** "Really need this to work" is loaded. Go straight in.

**Chloe:** I'm behind on rent. I'm in my overdraft. I thought if I could trade properly I could get on top of it quickly.

**Ștefan:** Damn, that sounds stressful. How long's it been like this?

> **PEEL (layer 1):** human first.

**Chloe:** About six months. It keeps getting worse.

**Ștefan:** I'm sorry, that's a lot. What are you doing for work at the minute?

**Chloe:** Retail, part-time. It barely covers it.

**Ștefan:** And roughly what are you hoping trading does for you, in terms of what you'd need?

> **[NODE: G]**

**Chloe:** A grand or two a month would sort me out. As quickly as possible.

**Ștefan:** As quickly as possible. Okay. Have you traded before, or would this be the start?

**Chloe:** Never traded. I've watched a few videos.

> **[NODE: QUAL, honest]** Time to be straight. Pressure plus no capital is a hard stop.

**Ștefan:** Right. I want to be upfront with you, because I'd rather be honest than just book you in. Is there any money set aside that you could risk without it affecting rent or bills?

**Chloe:** Not really. I was thinking of putting it on my credit card.

> **CURVEBALL: credit card.** HARD STOP. Clear, kind, final.

**Ștefan:** Okay. I'm going to be straight with you, Chloe, because I care how this goes for you. I wouldn't use a credit card for this, and I wouldn't use money you need for rent. Trading can lose money, and people who trade because they *have* to usually have a harder time, not an easier one. I don't think booking you in is the right move right now.

**Chloe:** *(quiet)* ...Okay.

**Ștefan:** I'm not saying never. If you can build a small cushion that isn't rent money, and the pressure has eased, you're welcome to come back, and I'd happily talk then. In the meantime, a free budgeting or debt advice service might get you further, faster, and a lot less risk. Would that be helpful?

**Chloe:** Yeah. I didn't think of that.

> Give a real next step. Don't just close the door. Point to a free, reputable debt advice service for her region.

**Ștefan:** Good. I really hope it turns around for you. And if things change, get back in touch. Take care of yourself.

### Why this works

- We stopped the framework when the pressure showed up and went human.
- We didn't treat "no" as a failure. A bad booking wastes the closer's time and could hurt her.
- The credit card hard stop was said once, kindly, and not argued.
