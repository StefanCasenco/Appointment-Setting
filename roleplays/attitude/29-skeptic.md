# 29 · Skeptic

**Layers on:** any experience path (here: Burned-adjacent beginner, PE → WGW → G → TL → CS)
**Lead:** Greg, 36, bus driver. Applied after his cousin said trading is a scam. Arms folded from the first word.
**Hidden truth:** He's not against trading, he's afraid of being made a fool of again. He wants someone to talk him out of his fear.
**Curveballs:** "Is this a scam?" in the first minute, and "how much are you making off me?"

---

## The call

**Ștefan:** Heeey Greg? Ștefan from [COMPANY], looks like you downloaded our futures guide, right?

**Greg:** Yeah. Before we start, is this one of those scams?

> **CURVEBALL (minute one).** Don't recite credentials. Agree with the instinct, lower the stakes.

**Ștefan:** Honestly, good to ask, there's a lot of junk out there. I'm not going to ask you for anything on this call. 10-15 minutes, I just want to understand where you're at, and if it makes sense, I'd connect you with someone who can explain properly. If it doesn't, I'll say so. Fair?

**Greg:** ...Fine.

**Ștefan:** What made you download the guide in the first place?

**Greg:** Curious. Cousin's into it, says it's all fake. Wanted to see for myself.

> **[NODE: INTENT]** "Curious" is real but thin. Use his cousin as the door in.

**Ștefan:** What does your cousin say exactly?

**Greg:** That he lost money and everyone does. That the people selling courses are the only ones who win.

**Ștefan:** He's right about a lot of that, honestly. Most people do lose, and plenty of sellers do cash in. What did you hope you'd find that was different?

**Greg:** I don't know. A way out of driving, maybe. I'm on the buses ten hours a day.

> **[NODE: G + CS]** The skepticism was armor. Now there's a real reason underneath.

**Ștefan:** What would a way out look like?

**Greg:** Making maybe 3k extra. Enough to do four days a week and be home.

**Ștefan:** What's stopped you looking into it properly?

> **[NODE: WGW]**

**Greg:** Fear of getting played. And my cousin's story.

**Ștefan:** So from what I understand, you drive ten hours a day, you'd love an extra 3k a month so you can cut a day and be home more, and what's stopped you is you don't want to get burned the way your cousin did, correct?

**Greg:** That's it.

**Ștefan:** And if you found something that was legitimate, would you actually want to pursue it?

**Greg:** If it was real. Yeah.

> **CURVEBALL: "How much do you make from this?"**

**Greg:** And what do *you* get out of this call?

**Ștefan:** Fair. I'm paid to talk to people and book calls with [CLOSER] when it's a fit. I don't get anything from pressuring you, and it's better for everyone if the wrong people don't book. Does that sit okay?

> Honest, short. Never deny the commercial side.

**Greg:** Okay. That's straight.

**Ștefan:** If it was legitimate, would you be ready to invest in yourself?

**Greg:** If I saw it was real, sure.

**Ștefan:** It's a real mentorship, not a $50 course, different programs, I wasn't trained on pricing. How much would you have available for something like this?

**Greg:** Maybe 2,000.

**Ștefan:** Good. [CLOSER] can answer every question you have, including the ones about your cousin. Free tomorrow or Thursday?

**Greg:** Thursday evening.

**Ștefan:** 6 or 7?   **Greg:** 7.

**Ștefan:** Locked in Thursday at 7. Best email?... Number?... Accept it now.

## Improv notes
- If he'd stayed hostile, ask once: "What would you need to see to feel okay talking to someone?" If he can't answer, end politely.
- Never say "trust me." Offer checkable things instead (the closer, questions, time).
- A skeptic who books is often a good student. A skeptic who won't answer personal questions isn't a lead yet.
